SIERRA BANCORP REPORTS RECORD QUARTERLY INCOME
Porterville, Calif. – (BUSINESS WIRE) – Sierra Bancorp (Nasdaq: BSRR), parent of Bank of the Sierra, today announced its unaudited financial results for the three- and nine-month periods ended September 30, 2026. Sierra Bancorp reported consolidated net income of $15.3 million, or $1.18 per diluted share, for the third quarter of 2026, compared to $9.7 million, or $0.72 per diluted share, in the third quarter of 2025. Return on average assets was 1.66% and return on average equity was 16.49% for the third quarter of 2026, compared to 1.04% and 10.81%, respectively, for the same period in 2025.
For the first nine months of 2026, the Company recognized net income of $37.7 million, or $2.90 per diluted share, as compared to $29.4 million, or $2.15 per diluted share, for the same period in 2025. The Company's improved financial performance metrics for the first nine months of 2026 include a net interest margin of 3.80% and an efficiency ratio of 56.73%, as compared to a net interest margin of 3.73% and efficiency ratio of 59.32% for the same period in 2025.

INCOME STATEMENT HIGHLIGHTS
Net Interest Income
Net interest income was $32.4 million for the third quarter of 2026, an increase of $0.4 million, or 1%, compared to the third quarter of 2025. The increase was primarily attributable to lower funding costs, higher loan yields, and the recovery of approximately $1.1 million of previously unrecognized interest income associated with an agricultural production nonaccrual loan that paid in full during the quarter. This credit relationship is unrelated to the agricultural production credit discussed in the Credit Loss Expense section. The favorable interest recovery was partially offset by lower average investment securities balances and yields. Interest expense declined $1.4 million, or 12%, from the prior-year quarter, reflecting lower rates on deposits and borrowed funds and a decrease in the average balance of borrowed funds.
For the third quarter of 2026, average interest-earning assets decreased $67.4 million, or 2%, from the same period in 2025, while the yield on those assets remained relatively stable at 5.17%, compared to 5.18% in the prior-year quarter. The decline in average earning assets was primarily attributable to lower investment securities balances, partially offset by growth in average commercial real estate and mortgage warehouse loan balances.
Average interest-bearing liabilities decreased $45.6 million, or 2%, compared to the third quarter of 2025, while the cost of those liabilities declined 21 basis points to 1.92%. The decrease in funding costs was primarily attributable to lower costs on interest-bearing deposits and borrowed funds. Average customer time deposits declined $42.2 million from the prior-year quarter and brokered deposit costs decreased 33 basis points, contributing to the improvement in overall funding costs.
The reduction in funding costs and improvement in loan yields more than offset modest pressure on earning asset balances, resulting in a 13 basis point increase in tax-equivalent net interest margin to 3.91%, compared to 3.78% in the third quarter of 2025.

Compared to the linked quarter, net interest income increased $2.0 million, or 6%, driven by higher earning asset yields and a favorable funding mix. The increase in loan yields was partially due to the recovery of approximately $1.1 million of previously unrecognized interest income associated with an agricultural production nonaccrual loan that paid in full during the third quarter. While deposit and borrowing costs increased modestly during the quarter, the overall cost of interest-bearing liabilities remained unchanged at 1.92% as average balances of higher-cost overnight and short-term borrowings declined $89.0 million. Average interest-earning assets increased $19.4 million, while the yield on those assets increased 15 basis points to 5.17%. Net interest margin expanded 17 basis points to 3.91% in the third quarter of 2026 from 3.74% in the second quarter of 2026.
The Company temporarily paused growth within its mortgage warehouse portfolio during the quarter in response to elevated interest rate volatility and changing conditions in the residential mortgage market. Enhanced reviews of our customer's credit risk profiles, including hedging positions and pipelines, resulted in a more selective approach to extending mortgage warehouse credit at the end of the third quarter 2026. Despite a decline of $97.1 million in period-end mortgage warehouse balances during the quarter, average mortgage warehouse balances decreased by only $20.8 million compared to the linked quarter, while related interest income declined slightly by $0.2 million, reflecting the continued profitability of the portfolio.
Net interest income for the first nine months of 2026 increased $0.7 million, or 1%, to $93.4 million, compared to the same period in 2025. The increase was primarily attributable to lower funding costs and the recovery of approximately $1.1 million of previously unrecognized interest income associated with a nonaccrual loan that paid in full during the third quarter, partially offset by lower average investment securities balances. Average interest-earning assets decreased $35.7 million, or 1%, while the tax-equivalent yield on earning assets declined seven basis points to 5.07%.
For the first nine months of 2026, interest expense decreased $3.7 million, or 10%, to $31.7 million. The decrease was driven by a 22 basis point reduction in the cost of interest-bearing liabilities to 1.93%, reflecting favorable repricing of deposits and wholesale funding balances. As a result, tax-equivalent net interest margin increased seven basis points to 3.80%, compared to 3.73% for the same period in 2025.
The Company had $1.8 billion in adjustable and variable rate loans and $196.9 million in floating rate bonds, as compared to $201.6 million in floating rate CDs and $36.1 million in floating rate trust preferred securities at September 30, 2026. Included in the $1.8 billion of adjustable and variable rate loans was approximately $360.3 million of mortgage warehouse facilities, or 15% of the Company's loan portfolio. Although these loans do not generally reprice while outstanding, their short duration and rapid turnover result in yields that adjust quickly to changes in market interest rates. Additionally, approximately $158.5 million of collateralized loan obligations within the available-for-sale investment security portfolio continue to provide asset sensitivity through periodic rate resets. In addition, there were $647.7 million of fixed-term deposits that reprice or mature within twelve months.
Credit Loss Expense
Credit loss expense on loans was a benefit of $2.0 million for the third quarter of 2026, compared to expense of $3.7 million for the third quarter of 2025, and expense of $2.2 million in the prior linked quarter. For the first nine months of 2026, credit loss expense on loans was $0.3 million, compared to $6.9 million for the same period in 2025.
The third quarter provision reversal was primarily attributable to the resolution of a previously identified agricultural production lending relationship in the lumber industry. During the second quarter of 2026, the Company established a $2.5 million specific reserve on the credit due to elevated repayment risk. During the third quarter, the credit relationship was resolved through the sale of the note to another creditor. As a result, the Company recovered substantially all principal, accrued interest, and fees associated with the relationship, resulting in a $0.1 million charge-off and the reversal of the previously established specific reserve. The resolution of this relationship contributed to a reduction in the allowance for credit losses on loans from $23.6 million at June 30, 2026, to $21.7 million at September 30, 2026. The nonaccrual agricultural production credit discussed in the Net Interest Income section is unrelated to the previously reserved agricultural production relationship in the lumber industry discussed in the Credit Loss Expense and Asset Quality sections. Excluding the impact of this previously reserved credit relationship, credit quality trends across the remainder of the loan portfolio were stable to improved during the quarter. Nonperforming assets declined to $9.3 million at September 30, 2026, from $14.8 million at December 31, 2025, classified loans declined to $27.0 million from $31.4 million, and loans past due 30 to 89 days improved to $2.5 million from $6.8 million at year-end 2025.
The Company recorded credit loss expense on unfunded commitments of $10,000 during the third quarter of 2026 and a benefit of $130,000 for the first nine months of 2026, compared to a benefit of $20,000 and expense of $80,000, respectively, during the comparable 2025 periods.

Compared to the third quarter of 2025, total noninterest income decreased $0.3 million, or 4%, from $8.1 million. The decrease was primarily attributable to a $0.5 million decline in earnings on separate-account life insurance, partially offset by increases in service charges and fees on deposit accounts and the above-mentioned special FHLB dividend.
For the first nine months of 2026, noninterest income increased $1.0 million, or 4%, to $24.3 million compared to $23.3 million for the same period in 2025. The increase was primarily driven by a $0.4 million gain on the sale of fixed assets, a $0.3 million increase in cash surrender value income from life insurance, a $0.3 million increase in service charges and fees on deposit accounts, and a $0.2 million increase in other income. These favorable variances were partially offset by the absence of securities gains recognized during the first nine months of 2025.
The Company’s non‑qualified deferred compensation plan for officers and directors allows participants to defer a portion of their earnings and select from various hypothetical investment alternatives to determine their individual returns. The Company economically offsets this liability with separate-account life insurance policies that are invested in similar underlying fund types within the life insurance policy. Because the deferred compensation liability and the separate-account life insurance asset are not contractually linked, differences in balances, fund performance, and insurance costs can result in temporary timing mismatches between changes in separate-account life insurance income and the related deferred compensation expense.

Balance Sheet Summary
Total assets decreased $154.1 million, or 4%, during the first nine months of 2026 to $3.68 billion at September 30, 2026. The decline was primarily attributable to a $118.3 million decrease in gross loans and a $53.7 million decrease in investment securities, partially offset by a $16.1 million increase in other assets. The increase in other assets was primarily driven by the Company's strategic investment in a renewable energy tax equity project, additional investments in low-income housing tax credit funds, and growth in other partnership investments designed to enhance long-term shareholder returns and provide current and future tax benefits.
The decrease in gross loan balances compared to December 31, 2025, was primarily driven by a $158.0 million decrease in mortgage warehouse facilities, partially offset by a $74.1 million increase in commercial real estate loans and continued loan production across the Company's core lending markets. The reduction in mortgage warehouse balances reflects a temporary pause in growth in the third quarter of 2026 due to the implementation of enhanced controls, as well as market volatility throughout 2026 due to the higher rate environment, while growth in commercial real estate lending demonstrates continued success in originating and retaining relationship-based commercial credits. We continue to add new mortgage warehouse customers with total commitments increasing $82.3 million, or 10.6%, during the first nine months of 2026. Most of the increase was in the third quarter with $54.4 million of net new commitments.
Despite the larger decline in period-end mortgage warehouse balances, average mortgage warehouse balances decreased by only $20.8 million during the third quarter of 2026 compared to the linked quarter, while related interest income declined slightly by $0.2 million. In addition, commercial real estate balances increased $75.3 million during the quarter, reflecting strong production activity and growth in relationship-based lending.
The Company's loan portfolio remains well diversified, with commercial real estate representing 60% of total loans, mortgage warehouse facilities representing 15%, residential real estate comprising 14%, and other commercial loans representing 7% of the portfolio at September 30, 2026. Commercial real estate lending continues to be a key area of growth, supported by strong customer demand and a healthy pipeline of opportunities.
As indicated in the loan rollforward table below, the Company significantly increased new credit extended, excluding changes in line of credit and mortgage warehouse utilization, during the quarter to $123.7 million as compared to $49.4 million in the previous quarter, representing the highest quarterly production level since the fourth quarter of 2020. This increase is due significantly to enhanced oversight through a collective semi-monthly review of credit opportunities. While credit activity increased, it was offset by significant declines in mortgage warehouse utilization as well as higher than normal paydowns. Paydowns, maturities, charge-offs and amortization totaled $84.7 million, including approximately $45.9 million from five large relationship payoffs and an additional $10.2 million from two significant principal reductions.

About Sierra Bancorp
Sierra Bancorp is the holding Company for Bank of the Sierra (www.bankofthesierra.com), which is in its 49th year of operations and strives to be the preeminent bank headquartered in the South San Joaquin Valley.
Bank of the Sierra offers a broad range of retail and commercial banking services through its 34 full-service branches located within the counties of Tulare, Kern, Kings, Fresno, Ventura, San Luis Obispo, and Santa Barbara. The Bank also maintains an online branch and provides specialized lending services through its mortgage warehouse division. Bank of the Sierra is recognized as one of the strongest and top-performing community banks in the country, with a 5-star rating from Bauer Financial.
