MetaVia Reports Third Quarter 2026 Financial Results and Provides Corporate Update
Positive DA-1726 16-Week Phase 1 Part 3 Interim Data Support Planned Phase 2 Development in MetALD and ALD
Appointed Sarah Browne, M.D., as Chief Medical Officer to Help Lead the Next Stage of Clinical Development for DA-1726 and Vanoglipel
Full Data From the Phase 1 Parts 3A and 3B Expected in the First Quarter of 2027
CAMBRIDGE, Mass., November 13, 2026 – MetaVia Inc. (Nasdaq: MTVA), a clinical-stage biotechnology company focused on transforming liver-related cardiometabolic diseases, today announced financial results for the third quarter ended September 30, 2026, and provided a corporate strategic update.
"The positive interim results from our Phase 1 Part 3B study of DA-1726, a novel oxyntomodulin (OXM) analog and dual GLP-1/glucagon receptor agonist, were recently highlighted in an oral presentation at the Precision Liver Symposium. We believe these findings provide important clinical support for DA-1726's potential to treat metabolic dysfunction and alcohol-associated liver disease (MetALD), as well as alcohol-associated liver disease (ALD)," stated Hyung Heon Kim, President and Chief Executive Officer of MetaVia. "Most notably, after approximately 16 weeks, we observed a 68.1% placebo-adjusted reduction in liver fat, with all five of the participants with baseline steatosis achieving MRI-PDFF below 5%. These findings were accompanied by 13.5% placebo-adjusted weight loss, significant reductions in adipose tissue and relative preservation of lean tissue. Recently reported additional interim data also demonstrated a statistically significant 1.47 mg/dL placebo-adjusted reduction in uric acid after 16 weeks of treatment, further supporting DA-1726's liver-focused cardiometabolic profile. Based on these results, we are planning a 48-week Phase 2 study in up to 150 subjects with MetALD and ALD, targeted to begin in the second half of 2027. We also recently hosted an invitation-only KOL dinner during AASLD's The Liver Meeting 2026 featuring Dr. Rohit Loomba, a member of our Scientific Advisory Board. The event provided a valuable opportunity to gather leading experts in the field to discuss the evolving treatment landscape in MetALD and the significant unmet medical need we hope to address."
Mr. Kim continued, "Last month, we also expanded our clinical leadership team with the appointment of Sarah Browne, M.D., as Chief Medical Officer. Sarah brings more than 20 years of experience spanning clinical development, regulatory science and clinical medicine, including leading the development of pemvidutide, a dual GLP-1/glucagon receptor agonist, through Phase 2 and establishing Phase 3 development strategies. Her extensive experience developing therapies for metabolic and liver diseases will be particularly valuable as we prepare to move DA-1726 into Phase 2 development in MetALD and ALD. In parallel, we continue to advance vanoglipel, our G-protein-coupled receptor 119 (GPR119) agonist, in combination with resmetirom for MASH, with a Phase 2a proof-of-concept study targeted to begin in the first half of 2027."

About MetaVia
MetaVia Inc. is a clinical-stage biotechnology company focused on transforming liver-related cardiometabolic diseases. The company is currently developing DA-1726 for the treatment of various cardiometabolic conditions, with potential in both Metabolic Dysfunction and Alcohol-Associated Liver Disease (MetALD) and Alcohol-Associated Liver Disease (ALD), and is advancing vanoglipel (DA-1241) in combination with resmetirom for the treatment of Metabolic Dysfunction-Associated Steatohepatitis (MASH). DA-1726 is a novel oxyntomodulin (OXM) analogue that functions as a glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor (GLP1R) and glucagon receptor (GCGR) dual agonist, engineered with a 3:1 GLP1R:GCGR potency ratio. OXM is a naturally-occurring gut hormone that activates GLP1R and GCGR, thereby decreasing food intake while increasing energy expenditure. In a Phase 1 Part 3B interim analysis at Day 109, DA-1726 demonstrated placebo-adjusted reductions of 13.5% in body weight and 68.1% in liver fat, along with reductions in visceral and subcutaneous adipose tissue and relatively preserved lean mass. A Phase 2, 48-week, non-biopsy study of DA-1726 in MetALD and ALD, is targeted to begin in the second half of 2027. Vanoglipel is a potential first-in-class drug candidate targeting G-protein-coupled receptor 119 (GPR119). It acts directly in the liver on pathways involved in steatosis, inflammation and fibrosis, while GPR119 activation in the pancreas and gut also supports glucose and metabolic regulation. In a Phase 2a clinical study, vanoglipel demonstrated direct hepatic action in addition to its glucose lowering effects. MetaVia is advancing vanoglipel in combination with resmetirom as a once-daily oral combination therapy for MASH, with a Phase 2a proof-of-concept study targeted to begin in the first half of 2027.
